02 / Calibration method

Real forces, explained with CAE evidence

The calibration began directly from real robot force measurements. Difficulties in that first attempt led us to use Ansys Motion to inspect the deformation mechanisms, identify a missing metal-wire load, and add an equivalent axial force to the Newton / MuJoCo system before calibrating the combined response against the real data.

STEP 1 / DIRECT REAL-DATA CALIBRATION

A · Fit the initial contact model

We first tried to match the measured insertion forces by tuning the existing plug and latch model directly against the real robot data. Adjusting stiffness, damping and contact parameters did not produce a satisfactory response across the observations: some candidates developed force spikes or unstable motion, while more stable configurations produced too little axial force.

The model was asking its existing latch and contact parameters to compensate for a load source that it did not represent. The stable contact-only baseline shown below reaches about 4.01 N, compared with real total-force peaks of 11.03–11.59 N; it is a reference result from this model, rather than a plot of every failed attempt.

STEP 2 / CAE MECHANISM IDENTIFICATION

Find the missing metal-wire deformation

We then used Ansys Motion to inspect what deforms during insertion. In addition to the upper latch, the metal contact wires inside the socket bend as the plug advances. This deformation, overlooked in the initial calibration model, contributes its own insertion resistance. See the CAE views below →

CAE was used to characterize the axial force contributed by these metal contacts separately from the latch. The available metal-load channel peaks at about 5.61 N and excludes the upper latch force. This component evidence gives the missing axial load a physical basis; it is not itself the approximately 11 N total force measured by the robot.

STEP 3 / COUPLED CALIBRATION

C · Add the axial load and recalibrate

We represented the missing metal-wire resistance with an equivalent axial force in Newton / MuJoCo. A depth-dependent load law is evaluated from the actual plug pose and applied during each dynamics step, so plug motion, latch deformation and contact forces all respond to the added load.

The equivalent-load parameters were calibrated against the real force evidence, and the combined model was rerun in coupled dynamics. The final candidate reaches about 11.12 N and meets the current axial-force, lateral-force and event-time criteria for all five development recordings. 03 / Physical model

Equivalent load and latch recovery

The added term supplies the axial resistance identified through CAE. In the current implementation, the equivalent load is written to Newton state.body_f at the plug root and passed to MuJoCo xfrc_applied at every substep. It acts as an applied load inside the dynamics; this version does not introduce a separate MuJoCo XML actuator. The final force response is recomputed by the solver, rather than formed by adding a curve after simulation.

The equivalent load follows F(s) = k × clip(s − s₀, 0, L), with k = 1383.238695 N/m, s₀ = 2.98159284 mm and fixed L = 10 mm. Depth is read from the actual plug pose at every substep. This is an effective axial model; its residual load cannot be uniquely attributed to metal elasticity.

In the latest candidate, the release drop comes from changing latch contact while the equivalent load continues to rise. This describes the candidate's internal mechanism, not an independently measured decomposition of the real release.

04 / Validation & traceability

What the evidence establishes

Observation criteria passed; qualification remains open. All five Piper repeats were used for development, with no independent holdout. The timestep feature change is 0.028135, above the existing 0.015 budget. Different speeds, withdrawal, pull-out locking and complete wrench behavior remain unvalidated for this candidate. The latest local force model has not been integrated into the full robot replay.
